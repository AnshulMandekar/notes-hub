05

Finding the numbers

parse_statement() always tries the tables first. Here the tables held only the period header, so it found 10 periods but no data, and fell back to reading the text line by line.

How one line becomes ten numbers

For every line after the header, the parser splits on spaces and walks from the right, collecting tokens while they look like numbers. The first token that is not a number marks the end of the label.

From 152 lines to 6 fields

Three things to notice:

  • The rules worked. No invented numbers, no advice, no banned words, and the risk label matches the calculation.
  • The numbers are not rounded. The code sends raw floats, so the LLM repeats "6.651633244244211". Rounding to 2 decimals before building the prompt would fix this.
  • The LLM faithfully explained wrong data. "Both asset growth and liability growth stand at 6.65%" sounds perfectly reasonable and is entirely a side effect of the parsing bug. The LLM cannot know; this is exactly why the credibility check exists, and why it should be able to stop a run.

Questions about this run

Walk me through what happens when you upload a Bloomberg PDF.

The bytes are saved to a temporary file and pdfplumber extracts each page's text and tables. The parser reads the company and currency from the header, tries the tables, and if they hold no data reads the text line by line: it finds the period header, then for each line reads numbers from the right and maps the remaining label to a field with regex. The builder merges everything into one JSON, writes it plus three agent-specific files, then the credibility checks and the readiness check run, and finally the agents calculate and the LLM explains.

In your TCS demo, the credibility score was 65. What caused that?

The accounting identity check failed in all ten years: liabilities had been overwritten by the "Total Liabilities & Equity" row, which equals total assets. That cost all 20 points. The rest was mostly passes plus a completeness warning, since a balance sheet has no revenue or profit fields. With a one-line regex fix the identity passes and the score is 90.

How did a parsing bug change the final risk label?

With liabilities equal to assets, liability growth exactly equalled asset growth (6.65%). That satisfied the "assets growing at least as fast as liabilities" condition, so both agents said low. The real liability growth was 16.16%, which makes both labels moderate.

Why did the Revenue agent fail on this PDF?

The file is a balance sheet only, so there is no revenue. The agent needs at least 4 periods and found 0, so it raised an error, which _safe_run stored. The fix is to upload the income statement as well; the app also should have warned about it in the readiness check.

Did the LLM make any mistakes?

It followed every rule: no invented numbers, no advice, and the labels matched. But it repeated unrounded numbers and confidently described wrong data. That shows the design is right to keep the LLM away from calculation, and that the quality of the explanation depends entirely on the input, which is why data checks matter.
