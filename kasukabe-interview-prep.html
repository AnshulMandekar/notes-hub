Say this first

Your pitch, in 60 seconds and in 3 minutes

60 seconds

"In the Amazon ML Challenge 2026, my team Kasukabe built a business entity-resolution system. We had a clean master list of 1.7 million businesses and 10 million noisy records from two other sources: typos, OCR digits, abbreviations, names in nine Indian scripts, missing addresses, and decoys that look like real businesses but aren't. The data covered the US and India, and the test added France, which never appeared in training. The metric was macro F0.5 per business, so precision counted twice as much as recall.

We normalised names and addresses with our own transliteration, generated candidates with four IDF-weighted sparse searches per country (144 million pairs, 98.7% recall), scored them with 80 pair features and a two-stage LightGBM, and gave each record to its single best business above a tuned threshold. We reached 0.9875 on held-out businesses and 0.9737 on the public leaderboard. The biggest lesson came from debugging the gap between those two numbers: statistics computed over a whole dataset, like IDF, silently change meaning when the country mix changes, and making them per-country fixed matches the model had been rejecting."

The 3-minute version adds four things

  1. Why blocking is the recall ceiling. 1.7 × 1013 possible pairs; four searches (name + address top 10, name top 5, address top 5, character 3-grams top 1) plus candidates borrowed from look-alike records keep 98.71% of true pairs, so a perfect matcher could reach about 0.996.
  2. Why two stages. Stage 1 judges each pair alone. Stage 2 looks at each record's best candidate in context: how far ahead of the runner-up it is, how many other records chose the same business, and whether those "sibling" records agree with this one on house number and extra words. That added about 0.004.
  3. Why the threshold is 0.85, not the validation optimum (~0.7). The test has about one extra decoy per business. Uploads showed 0.69 < 0.85 and 0.85 > strict 0.95–0.97, so we settled at 0.85, plus a stricter bar for "next-door" house numbers.
  4. What you would do next. A small cross-encoder re-ranker for the uncertain band, fixing a small leak in the stage-2 split, and learning French noise words.

Numbers that differ between files

The write-up says "~95 features"; the code has 80 stage-1 features (features.FEATURES) and stage 2 adds 25 (8 context + 17 sibling). Say "80 pair features, plus 25 context features in stage 2".

Recall is 98.71% for the final configuration with the character search; the v16 log shows 98.60% (no character pass). The ceiling is 0.99591 in the write-up and 0.99554 in the v16 log. The v16 predict log has 140M pairs; the final run with the character pass has 144,041,669. If asked, explain which run each number comes from.
